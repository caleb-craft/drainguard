The Problem

When waste ends up in the wrong place, drains pay the price

Plastic and other discarded waste can collect around drainage channels and slow the flow of water. This can contribute to blockages and flooding during heavy rain.

Illustrative image, not a specific location

01

Waste around drainage channels

Bottles, bags and wrappers dropped near drains can be pushed in by rain or wind and pile up at the opening, narrowing the path for water.

Illustrative image, not a specific location

02

Plastic pollution

Plastic breaks down very slowly. When it is not collected and recycled, it can stay in the environment for a long time and split into smaller pieces.

Illustrative image, not a specific location

03

Waterways affected by waste

Water moving through drains and channels can carry waste with it. Some gets caught along channel edges or flows on into rivers and lagoons, harming the environment around them.

Illustrative image, not a specific location

04

Flooding-related concerns

When drains are blocked, rainwater can build up on roads and around homes. Waste is one of several factors, alongside heavy rain, drain capacity and maintenance.

Our Solution

Three parts working towards one goal

DrainGuard is designed as a connected system. Each part is at a different stage, shown honestly below.

Prototype in progress

Smart Recycling Bin

A physical bin built to detect deposited items using an ESP32 microcontroller, an ultrasonic sensor and a servo-controlled flap.

Interface in progress

Connected Website

A website that will link the recycling experience to a person's activity and account. Secure accounts and live data still need a backend.

Planned

Recycling Rewards

A planned feature that tracks qualifying recycling activity and calculates points. Money, mobile data and school materials are possible rewards, none live yet.

How It Works

From bin to reward in six steps

The intended journey of a recycled item through the DrainGuard system.

  1. 01

    Deposit

    A user puts a recyclable item into the designated bin.

  2. 02

    Detect

    The bin's sensor and programmed logic detect that an item was dropped in.

  3. 03

    Scan

    The user scans the bin's QR code to open the DrainGuard website.

  4. 04

    Track

    The website is intended to link a verified recycling event to the right account.

  5. 05

    Earn

    Eligible events add points to the account under the project's reward rules.

  6. 06

    Redeem

    In future, points could be redeemed for supported rewards.

Scanning the QR code alone never earns points

Points are only meant to be awarded after the bin itself verifies a deposit, so a scan on its own cannot be used to claim rewards. This verification link between the bin and the website belongs to the planned backend and is not built yet.

Recycling Information

What the bin is meant to accept

The prototype starts with plastic. Other materials can be added only if the bin can genuinely handle them.

Accepted in prototype

Plastic bottles

Empty drink and water bottles. The main focus of the first prototype.

Not accepted yet

Paper and cardboard

May be considered later if the bin and collection process can support it.

Not accepted yet

Metal cans

A possible future category. The current design does not handle metal.

Not accepted yet

Glass

Glass is heavy and can break, so it is outside the first prototype.

Important: the bin's sensor can tell that something was dropped in, but not what material it is. For the prototype, accepted items are set by the bin's rules and signage. Automatic material recognition has not been built or tested.

About DrainGuard

Making the right choice easier

Waste that is thrown away carelessly can end up around drains and channels. DrainGuard explores a simple idea: if putting recyclables in the right place is easy and rewarding, more people will do it.

A sensor can notice when an item is dropped into the bin, and a connected website can later link that event to a person's account. Together they could encourage better habits.

The intended impact is fewer items near drains, more material collected for recycling and more people thinking about where their waste goes. These are goals, not measured results.

DrainGuard is a school science and technology project.
